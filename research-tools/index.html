How this section works

The rest of feequity is about what it costs to hold a portfolio: broker fees, spreads, currency conversion, the settings nobody opens. This section is about the other half of the bill, the part you choose deliberately. A research subscription is a fixed annual cost sitting on a variable portfolio, which means it behaves exactly like a fund expense ratio and gets cheaper in basis points the more you have invested. That framing decides more of the buying question than any feature table, and almost nobody applies it.

Three rules govern everything published here.

Prices come from the vendor

Every figure is read from a page owned by the company selling the product, and stamped with the date. Where a company does not publish its price to a logged-out visitor, we record that as a finding rather than repeating a number from a review site.

Nothing is reviewed unused

No page is written before the product has been used in a logged-in session, and every feature claim carries an original screenshot with its capture date.

Commercial links are named

Where a link earns us a commission, that is disclosed on the same line as the link rather than once in a footer. Products we hold no relationship with are never linked commercially.

What we are paid, plainly

One product in this section currently earns us a commission: InvestingPro, the premium subscription of Investing.com, through an affiliate partnership. Investing.com does not see these pages before publication and has no approval over their contents. The other products covered here pay us nothing.

That creates an obvious pull on any comparison, and the defence against it is published rather than promised. The ranking weights on the alternatives page were fixed while exactly one product paid us anything, and two of the five criteria, worth forty percent between them, are the two on which that product scores worst. Start with the cost, not the features

Before you pay for research, work out what your broker already charges you each year. It is usually the larger number, and no research platform is incentivised to show it to you.
